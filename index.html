رحلة العمرة

The journey, stage by stage

Umrah has a fixed order — four pillars, in sequence. This overview gives you the shape of the whole trip; the next section opens each stage with its exact wording.

١

Ihram

الإحرام

At or before the miqat: ghusl, the ihram garments, the intention, and the Talbiyah — the state you enter and stay in until the final trim.

٢

Tawaf

الطواف

Seven circuits of the Ka'bah beginning at the Black Stone, then two rak'ahs near Maqam Ibrahim and a drink of Zamzam.

٣

Sa'i

السعي

Seven passes between Safa and Marwah, retracing Hajar's search for water — the walk that gave Zamzam its name.

٤

Halq or Taqsir

الحلق أو التقصير

Shaving or trimming the hair closes Umrah and releases you from ihram — the visit is complete.

Every step, with its wording

Each stage below has its Arabic text, transliteration and English meaning — tap to open it, then press Listen to hear it spoken aloud.

Perform ghusl — a full purifying bath, the Prophet's ﷺ own practice before entering ihram — then wear the ihram garments (unstitched white cloths for men; plain, modest clothing for women — the face and hands remain uncovered), make your intention, and begin reciting the Talbiyah, which you repeat often until you start Tawaf.

Intention (niyyah) for Umrah

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أُرِيدُ الْعُمْرَةَ فَيَسِّرْهَا لِي وَتَقَبَّلْهَا مِنِّي

Allāhumma innī urīdul-'umrata fa yassirhā lī wa taqabbalhā minnī

O Allah, I intend to perform Umrah — make it easy for me, and accept it from me.

Intention is an act of the heart; this wording is a commonly taught form, not a fixed recitation.

The Talbiyah

لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ

Labbayk Allāhumma labbayk, labbayka lā sharīka laka labbayk, innal-ḥamda wan-ni'mata laka wal-mulk, lā sharīka lak

Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. Verily all praise, grace and dominion are Yours. You have no partner.

Sahih al-Bukhari & Sahih Muslim. Men recite it aloud; women say it quietly.

Enter with the right foot, as with any mosque, and lower your gaze in humility as the Ka'bah comes into view. Many raise their hands and make personal du'a at first sight — there's no single wording required for that moment; speak to Allah in your own words.

Entering the mosque

اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ لِي أَبْوَابَ رَحْمَتِكَ

Allāhumma-ftaḥ lī abwāba raḥmatik

O Allah, open the gates of Your mercy for me.

Sahih Muslim — the general dua for entering any mosque.

Begin level with the Black Stone (Hajar al-Aswad), Ka'bah on your left. Say the takbir at the start of each of the seven circuits — kissing or touching the stone if possible, otherwise gesturing toward it without pushing. Between the Yemeni Corner and the Black Stone, recite the verse below; elsewhere, any dhikr or personal du'a is fine. Close with two rak'ahs near Maqam Ibrahim (Surah al-Kafirun, then al-Ikhlas) and a drink of Zamzam.

Starting each circuit

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

Bismillāh, wallāhu akbar

In the name of Allah, and Allah is the Greatest.

Between the Yemeni Corner and the Black Stone

رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

Rabbanā ātinā fid-dunyā ḥasanatan wa fil-ākhirati ḥasanatan wa qinā 'adhāban-nār

Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.

Qur'an 2:201, repeated by the Prophet ﷺ on this exact stretch of Tawaf (Sunan Abu Dawud).

After Tawaf, at Maqam Ibrahim

وَاتَّخِذُوا مِنْ مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ مُصَلًّى

Wattakhidhū min maqāmi Ibrāhīma muṣallā

And take the station of Ibrahim as a place of prayer.

Qur'an 2:125 — recited before the two rak'ahs. Pray them anywhere in the mosque if the area is crowded.

Approach Safa reciting the verse below, then climb it, face the Ka'bah, and say the takbir three times followed by the dhikr — repeating the whole sequence three times with your own du'a in between. Walk to Marwah (this is one circuit), repeat the same dhikr there, and continue for seven one-way trips in total, ending at Marwah.

Approaching Safa

إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِنْ شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ ﴿١٥٨﴾ أَبْدَأُ بِمَا بَدَأَ اللَّهُ بِهِ

Innaṣ-ṣafā wal-marwata min sha'ā'irillāh — abda'u bimā bada'a Allāhu bih

Indeed, Safa and Marwah are among the symbols of Allah. I begin with what Allah began with.

Qur'an 2:158, with the Prophet's ﷺ own opening words (Sahih Muslim).

On Safa and on Marwah

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ وَهُوَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ، أَنْجَزَ وَعْدَهُ وَنَصَرَ عَبْدَهُ وَهَزَمَ الْأَحْزَابَ وَحْدَهُ

Lā ilāha illallāhu waḥdahu lā sharīka lah, lahul-mulku wa lahul-ḥamdu yuḥyī wa yumītu wa huwa 'alā kulli shay'in qadīr. Lā ilāha illallāhu waḥdah, anjaza wa'dahu wa naṣara 'abdahu wa hazamal-aḥzāba waḥdah

There is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. His is the dominion and His is all praise; He gives life and causes death, and He has power over all things. There is no god but Allah alone, who fulfilled His promise, granted victory to His servant, and defeated the enemy alliance alone.

Sahih Muslim — said three times, facing the Qiblah, with personal du'a after each repetition.

Drinking Zamzam

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ عِلْمًا نَافِعًا، وَرِزْقًا وَاسِعًا، وَشِفَاءً مِنْ كُلِّ دَاءٍ

Allāhumma innī as'aluka 'ilman nāfi'an, wa rizqan wāsi'an, wa shifā'an min kulli dā'

O Allah, I ask You for beneficial knowledge, ample provision, and healing from every illness.

A widely used form drawn on the hadith "Zamzam water is for whatever it is drunk for" (Ibn Majah) — make it your own du'a, this need not be recited verbatim.

Men shave the head completely (halq) or trim evenly all over (taqsir); the Prophet ﷺ prayed for those who shave three times and for those who trim once — shaving is the more complete option for men. Women cut roughly a fingertip's length from a gathered lock of hair. Once done, ihram restrictions end and your Umrah is complete — no fixed du'a is prescribed for this step itself.

What about Tawaf al-Wada' (the farewell tawaf)? It's obligatory before leaving Makkah after Hajj, but for Umrah alone most scholars consider it recommended rather than required — Umrah's own rites end at halq or taqsir. Many people still perform it as a graceful way to say goodbye to the Ka'bah before travelling home; skipping it after Umrah isn't missing a pillar.
A note on authenticity. Every Arabic text above comes from the Qur'an or from hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abu Dawud or Sunan Ibn Majah, referenced beside each one. Where scholars differ on wording or where no fixed text exists, that's noted rather than papered over. This page is a study companion, not a fatwa — for rulings on your specific situation, ask a qualified local scholar.

Saudi regulations for Umrah travellers

A summary of how entry and pilgrimage permits currently work. Rules are set and updated by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Hajj and Umrah — treat this as an orientation and always re-check the Nusuk platform before you book or travel.

Route 1

Saudi Tourist e-Visa

  • Multiple entries, valid one year, up to 90 days per entry
  • Open to citizens of dozens of eligible countries; fully online, usually issued within a few days
  • Lets you perform Umrah and travel elsewhere in the Kingdom on the same trip
  • Still requires a separate Umrah permit through Nusuk before entering Masjid al-Haram
Route 2

Official Umrah Visa

  • Issued through licensed travel agents, dedicated to religious pilgrimage
  • Bundled with an approved service package (accommodation, transport) purchased via Nusuk
  • Traditional route for travellers who prefer a packaged trip
Newer option

One-year multiple-entry Umrah visa

  • Valid 365 days from issue; cumulative stay across all visits capped at 90 days
  • A new service package must be bought via Nusuk before each entry, and matched by a fresh Umrah permit
  • Visa pauses when you leave the Kingdom and resumes on your next entry; unused days carry forward
  • Not valid during the Hajj season (1 Dhul Qa'dah – 13 Dhul Hijjah)
The rule that applies to everyone: whichever visa gets you into the country, you still need a separate, timed Umrah permit issued through the Nusuk app before you can enter Masjid al-Haram — and a further permit if you want to visit Al-Rawdah in Madinah. Entry windows, stay durations and health requirements (such as vaccination rules) are amended periodically by the Ministry, so confirm the current terms on Nusuk shortly before you travel rather than relying on any single source, including this one.

The Nusuk App

Nusuk is Saudi Arabia's official platform for pilgrims — the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah's single point for permits, bookings and on-the-ground support. Registration takes your passport or visa details, a verification code sent to your phone, and a password; once your account is active, everything below sits inside it.

Umrah permit

A timed entry slot to Masjid al-Haram — mandatory for every pilgrim before entering, regardless of visa type.

Rawdah appointment

A separate booking for the small, high-demand area inside Masjid an-Nabawi known as Al-Rawdah Al-Sharifah.

Companion management

Add family members or children to your account so their permits move together with yours.

Ziyarat reservations

Optional visits to historic sites around Makkah and Madinah, booked the same way as your main permits.

Transport & stay

Haramain High-Speed Rail tickets, hotel accommodation and flight bookings through approved providers.

In the moment

Live crowd-density readings, maps and guided tours, the Qur'an, collected du'as, prayer times and Qiblah direction.

Getting registered. Download Nusuk (iOS or Android) or use the web platform, choose your pilgrim type (tourist e-visa holder or Umrah-visa holder), and enter your passport or visa number, date of birth and nationality. A one-time code arrives by SMS and email — enter both to activate the account, then set your password. Keep your registered phone number reachable while abroad; that's where Nusuk sends permit confirmations and codes.

Booking a visit to Al-Rawdah

The area between the Prophet's ﷺ pulpit and his house — described in hadith as one of the gardens of Paradise — admits visitors only by timed permit. Booking is free; the whole process runs through Nusuk, and any paid third-party "booking service" should be treated with suspicion.

1

Confirm your account is complete

Your Nusuk profile and passport details need to be fully verified before Rawdah booking becomes available — an incomplete profile is the most common reason the option won't open.

2

Go to Services → Book Rawdah Visit

It sits apart from the Umrah permit screen — a frequent first-time mix-up is booking one and assuming it covers both.

3

Pick a date and time slot

Slots typically release 7–14 days ahead (tightening to 3–5 days during Ramadan and the Hajj season), so an empty calendar further out is normal, not an error.

4

Confirm to receive your QR permit

The confirmed appointment and its QR code appear under My Profile → My Bookings, and are also emailed to your registered address as a backup.

5

Arrive within your window with the QR ready

Stewards scan the code at the entry point; without a valid permit, entry to the Rawdah itself isn't permitted, though the rest of Masjid an-Nabawi remains open.

If something isn't working: log out and back in to refresh a blank bookings screen; enter your mobile number with its full international dialling code if a verification code isn't arriving, wait 10–15 minutes before requesting another, and use Nusuk's in-app live chat or its 24-hour support line as a last resort. Nusuk has also added an instant-booking option for pilgrims already near the mosque with capacity available, plus a waitlist for fully booked slots.

Staying connected

You'll want data for Nusuk, maps and calling home — here's how pilgrims usually get online in Saudi Arabia.

STC

Sawa Ziyara (visitor line)

  • Visitor packages start around SAR 35
  • Can be reserved online in advance on the STC website
  • Activation on arrival requires a fingerprint at an STC sales point — this is a Saudi-wide rule, not an STC quirk
MBL

Visitors package

  • Packages start around SAR 30
  • Available at Mobily branches, airport kiosks and authorised shops
  • Straightforward walk-up purchase with your passport
ZN

Visitor SIM

  • Packages start around SAR 34.5
  • Kiosks at both Jeddah and Madinah airport arrival halls
  • Data-heavy plans aimed at tourists and pilgrims
1

Decide physical SIM or eSIM before you fly

A physical SIM means a short stop at the airport; an eSIM (from a Saudi carrier or an international eSIM marketplace) can be bought online, installed via QR code before departure, and left ready to switch on.

2

Physical SIM: buy it at the arrival hall

STC, Mobily and Zain all run kiosks at Jeddah's King Abdulaziz International and Madinah's Prince Mohammad bin Abdulaziz International — have your passport ready and expect a short fingerprint or biometric step to activate the line, which Saudi telecom rules require of every provider.

3

eSIM: activate remotely if you'd rather skip the queue

Saudi Arabia now lets international pilgrims activate an eSIM straight through a carrier's app using biometric verification via the Absher platform — no store visit needed, which is useful when airport counters are busy during peak Umrah season.

4

Top up as you go

All three carriers let you recharge through their apps, at convenience stores, or by USSD code — keep a little local cash for the first top-up in case a card payment doesn't go through.

Good to know: queues at airport SIM counters can run long during Ramadan and peak Umrah weeks, so an eSIM installed before departure is worth considering if you want to be reachable the moment you land. Whichever route you take, register the SIM to your own passport — Saudi law ties every active line to a verified identity.

Seasons, weather, and why timing matters

Umrah has no fixed date on the solar calendar — the Islamic year is lunar, so your trip's Gregorian date shifts by about 10–11 days earlier each year. That means weather and crowd size are two separate things to plan around, and they don't always move together.

June – September

Summer

  • Daytime highs regularly pass 42–45°C in both Makkah and Madinah, with record spikes above 50°C
  • Dry heat, but the mataf courtyard and outdoor queues offer little shade at midday
  • Fewer visitors than winter — if you can handle the heat, permits and hotels are easier to get
December – February

Winter

  • Daytime temperatures around 25–30°C, dropping to a cool 15–18°C at night
  • The most comfortable stretch of the year for standing and walking outdoors
  • Also the most crowded — comfortable weather draws the largest volume of visitors
Mar–May & Oct–Nov

Spring & autumn

  • A middle ground: warm days (30–38°C) without winter's peak crowding
  • Occasional short, heavy rain in the shoulder months, mainly near Jeddah's coast — rare, but can cause local flash flooding
  • Often the best balance of manageable weather and manageable queues
Lunar, not seasonal

Ramadan

The busiest single stretch on the calendar, regardless of what the weather is doing outside. Hotel prices climb, flights fill up months ahead, and — as noted earlier — Nusuk permit and Rawdah slots tighten to just 3–5 days out. If Ramadan is your goal, book earlier than you think you need to.

1 Dhul Qa'dah – 13 Dhul Hijjah

The pre-Hajj & Hajj window

Umrah visas and permits pause during this stretch so the Kingdom can focus on Hajj. Hajj itself is expected around late May 2026, and will keep drifting earlier through the Gregorian calendar in the years after — check the current Hijri dates before you plan around it.

Planning around both calendars. Pick a Gregorian window with weather you can manage, then check where it falls in the Hijri calendar for Ramadan and the Hajj blackout. If your dates land in summer, lean harder on the sun protection and hydration items in the packing checklist below, plan Tawaf and Sa'i for early morning or after Isha when it's cooler, and rest indoors during peak afternoon heat.

Famous places worth knowing

Beyond the two mosques, both cities hold sites tied closely to the Seerah. Many are optional ziyarat stops rather than rites of Umrah itself.

Masjid al-Haram

The Grand Mosque surrounding the Ka'bah — the direction every Muslim faces in prayer, and the heart of both Umrah and Hajj.

Jabal al-Nour & Cave of Hira

The mountain where the Prophet ﷺ received the first revelation inside the cave of Hira, reached by a steep climb east of the Haram.

Jabal Thawr

South of the city, where the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr sheltered in a cave during the migration to Madinah while being pursued.

Mina

The tented valley where Hajj pilgrims stay for several days — a short distance from the Haram, quiet outside the Hajj season.

Mount Arafat

Where the Prophet ﷺ gave his Farewell Sermon; standing here is the single obligatory pillar of Hajj, though it lies outside Umrah.

Muzdalifah

The open plain between Arafat and Mina where Hajj pilgrims gather pebbles and spend the night under open sky.

Masjid an-Nabawi

The Prophet's ﷺ Mosque in the heart of Madinah, built around his original house and expanded into one of the world's largest mosques.

Al-Rawdah Al-Sharifah

The garden of Paradise on earth, described in hadith — the space between the Prophet's ﷺ pulpit and his house, now permit-only.

Quba Mosque

The first mosque in Islamic history, founded by the Prophet ﷺ on arrival in Madinah — praying two rak'ahs here carries the reward of an Umrah, per hadith.

Masjid al-Qiblatain

The Mosque of the Two Qiblahs, where the direction of prayer changed mid-salah from Jerusalem to Makkah.

Mount Uhud

Site of the second major battle of early Islam; the martyrs' cemetery at its foot, including Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib, is a common ziyarat stop.

Jannat al-Baqi

Madinah's historic cemetery beside the Prophet's Mosque, resting place of many of his family and companions.

التسوق والمطاعم

Shopping & famous eateries

Where pilgrims usually pick up gifts, essentials and a good meal, near both mosques.

ABM

Abraj Al Bait (Clock Tower) Mall

A vast mall directly beside Masjid al-Haram — prayer wear, abayas, perfumes, dates and souvenirs across several floors, with mosque views from the upper levels.

SZ

Souq al-Zal

An older, closer-in market for prayer beads, oud and attar (alcohol-free perfume), abayas and modest wear at more traditional prices.

B&P

Bin Dawood & Panda supermarkets

Reliable chains scattered around the Haram for dates, Zamzam packs, snacks and everyday essentials at fixed prices.

ALB

Al Baik

The fried-chicken chain pilgrims from around the world talk about — queues are part of the experience, especially near Haram branches after Isha.

M&K

Mandi & Kabsa houses

Family-style rice-and-meat restaurants around Ajyad and Ibrahim al-Khalil streets — generous plates meant for sharing.

CT

Clock Tower rooftop cafés

Coffee and light meals with a direct view down onto the Haram and the Ka'bah — worth the elevator queue at least once.

ANM

Al Noor Mall

Directly facing Masjid an-Nabawi's King Fahd Gate — a straightforward stop for clothing, perfume and gifts between prayers.

DTS

Madinah date markets

Stalls and shops around the mosque and near Quba selling Ajwa and other Madinah dates by the box — a favourite gift to carry home.

T&B

Taibah & Bab al-Majidi markets

Older souq streets a short walk from the mosque, good for prayer mats, modest clothing and everyday shopping at local prices.

A&T

Al Baik & Al Tazaj

The same beloved fried-chicken and grilled-chicken chains familiar from Makkah, with branches close to the Haram in Madinah too.

MM

Madani Mandi restaurants

Local mandi and mutabbaq maqluba spots around the mosque's southern side — hearty, affordable, and popular after Fajr and Isha.

CH

Cafés along Al Haram Street

Quiet cafés facing the mosque's courtyards — a good place to sit with tea between the five daily prayers.
